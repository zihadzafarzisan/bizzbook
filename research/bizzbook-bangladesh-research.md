# Bizzbook: a social network for business in Bangladesh

Research date: 15 November 2026.

Further research: [Round 2 — adoption, buyer value, and network design](bizzbook-research-round-2.md) adds a close historical precedent, Anar, and qualifies the early conversation metric below. Use the reports together; positive social-commerce evidence does not establish demand for a new network.

## Product direction confirmed by the founder

Bizzbook is a Facebook-like app for people doing business with one another in Bangladesh. Factory owners, garment manufacturers, wholesalers, and new entrepreneurs create profiles, publish posts, discover products and opportunities in a feed, and communicate through DMs. The long-term scope covers products across categories.

Here, “P2P business” means direct relationships between people representing businesses, including entrepreneurs who are just starting. The commercial relationships will often be B2B. This does not imply consumer secondhand sales, lending, or a particular payment system.

The primary product is the business network. Catalogues, quotations, and transaction features can support that network as demand becomes clear.

## Assessment

The concept is worth validating. Bangladesh provides evidence that people already use social feeds and conversations for commerce. The unproven opportunity is whether a dedicated business network gives them enough additional value to build another habit and another network.

Proposed positioning: **Discover products, meet suppliers, and build business relationships across Bangladesh.**

The strongest initial promise to test is access to relevant, responsive business contacts with enough information to judge an opportunity. A familiar feed helps adoption; its contents and the quality of relationships must give users a reason to return.

## What the evidence supports

**Social commerce is an established behaviour.** The World Bank’s October 2021 Bangladesh Country Gender Assessment describes entrepreneurs using Facebook and WhatsApp to showcase products and accept orders, including women overcoming mobility and network constraints. This is historical context, not a current market-size estimate. [World Bank assessment, p. 41](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099062723021540392/pdf/P1688741e792dc2319aab142781baea1ec771bcce357.pdf)

**Trust and human relationships deserve product attention.** A 2025 study based on 37 interviews with Bangladeshi sellers, buyers, and stakeholders reports the importance of human interaction in informal e-markets. It supports preserving conversation and seller identity in Bizzbook’s experience. It does not establish willingness to switch platforms. [DAIEM research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12910)

**Accountability needs operations as well as interface features.** A 2026 research preprint studied informal commerce through a survey of 124 participants and 36 stakeholder interviews, then evaluated a reporting and mediation system with 32 participants. Its findings make reporting, evidence, moderation, and dispute handling relevant research areas. This is a small research sample, not national prevalence data or proof that the proposed system scales. [Bonik Somiti preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12650)

**Product quality, service, and price matter alongside social engagement.** A 2025 qualitative study of 41 Dhaka consumers buying rice and shrimp through Facebook found these purchase criteria and the influence of reviews and recommendations. Its consumer/agri-food scope limits application to wholesale garments or other Bizzbook categories. [Sharmin et al., 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/isd2.70021)

These sources support the relevance of social commerce and trust. They do not validate Bizzbook’s demand, customer acquisition cost, retention, or revenue model.

## People and relationships to design around

One person may buy materials, sell finished products, and look for distribution partners. Accounts should support multiple business roles rather than permanently separating all members into buyers and sellers.

| Participant | Main job to investigate | Useful content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Factory or garment owner / authorized representative | Find suitable buyers and communicate capabilities | Production videos, stock offers, capabilities, capacity updates |
| Wholesaler or distributor | Reach resellers and maintain repeat relationships | New arrivals, quantity pricing, availability, restock notices |
| First-time entrepreneur | Find a manageable first purchase and understand the supplier | Small-order offers, samples, clear quantities, questions and answers |
| Existing small retailer or online seller | Discover products and maintain dependable contacts | Saved suppliers, restocks, buyer requests, repeat enquiries |

A new entrepreneur should be able to participate before owning a registered company. Verification labels should explain exactly what has been checked, rather than treating every member as equally verified.

## The factory–beginner mismatch

Direct factory access does not automatically mean an order a beginner can afford. Garments Park BD’s published company profile lists an MOQ of 2,000 pieces per colour per style. This is one supplier example, not an industry average or a confirmed current quotation. [Company profile, p. 11](https://garmentspark.ssfashionwear.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Garments-Park-BD-Ltd-Company-profile-new.pdf)

Recommended product implications:

- Show manufacturer, wholesaler, distributor, and entrepreneur roles clearly; verify claims separately.
- Distinguish ready stock, custom production, and stock lots.
- For sale posts, encourage a price or price range, minimum quantity, location, stock status, and dispatch or production time.
- Show whether an MOQ applies to the whole order, each style, or each colour.
- Let buyers ask for samples and publish sourcing requests.

Wholesalers may provide useful small-batch access between factories and beginners. Their usefulness should be tested rather than assuming every relationship must eliminate intermediaries.

## Proposed first version

These are recommendations for validation, not features already implemented or approved specifications.

1. **Profiles and business identity.** A person’s name, business name where applicable, role, category, location, description, and business photos. A business can have authorized representatives; avoid shared passwords.
2. **A business feed.** Following and discovery feeds with photos, short videos, and text. Start with explicit interests, location, recency, and followed accounts to make relevance understandable.
3. **Posts for both sides.** Product offers, “looking for supplier” requests, business introductions, and useful business questions. Structured fields should help commercial posts while ordinary business posts remain easy to publish.
4. **Social interaction.** Follow, comment, react, save, and share. A post should lead naturally to the author’s business identity and a conversation.
5. **DMs attached to context.** Open a conversation from a post with that post linked. Support photos, simple enquiry prompts, notifications, message requests, and blocking/reporting. Test voice messages for Bangla-speaking users before prioritizing them.
6. **Search and filters.** Search people, businesses, posts, and products; filter by category and location, with quantity/ready-stock filters where posts contain those fields.
7. **Moderation tools.** Report queues, duplicate/spam controls, account restrictions, appeals, and clear rules for business-relevant content.

Test Bangla-first copy with English support, lightweight media, and simple onboarding with the intended users. These are design hypotheses, not confirmed language or device preferences.

An example journey: a garment owner posts a ready-stock offer → an entrepreneur discovers it → checks the profile and terms → asks a question or sends a DM → discusses a sample and purchase → follows the supplier for future arrivals.

Checkout, escrow, lending, warehousing, and owned delivery add operational responsibilities. Evaluate them after observing how members arrange real deals. A basic quotation or agreed-terms summary could be a useful intermediate step when conversations demonstrate the need.

## Keeping an all-category network relevant

The long-term scope can remain broad. Each member’s feed should reflect their business interests and relationships, so someone sourcing garments sees relevant offers and requests rather than a random mix of factory output.

For launch, recruit a few connected communities where buyers and suppliers already trade. Garments and adjacent categories are candidates given the founder’s emphasis, but final recruitment should follow access to credible suppliers and actual buyer demand. This is a way to populate useful relationships while keeping the platform open to the broader vision.

Category expansion requires category-specific information. Apparel may need size ratios and fabric details; food may need storage and expiry information; machinery may need specifications and warranty terms. One universal product form will not answer every buyer’s questions.

## Trust, safety, and commercial boundaries

Use distinct labels such as phone confirmed, business documents reviewed, and factory location visited, each with scope and date. A phone check cannot support a “verified factory” claim. Do not sell trust badges as advertising placements.

Start with transparent identity, message requests, fraud reporting, moderation, and clear explanations of Bizzbook’s role when users arrange transactions themselves. Reviews should distinguish a social endorsement from a purchase-backed review; off-platform purchases are harder to verify. Identity documents should remain private with controlled access.

Before adding payment collection or guarantees, resolve the settlement model, refunds, provider support, and operational responsibility. bKash advertises online business payment services, and Pathao offers courier, COD, tracking, and returns. Their availability does not confirm support for Bizzbook’s multi-seller settlement model or every product/parcel type. [bKash online business](https://www.bkash.com/en/business/online-business), [Pathao Courier](https://pathao.com/courier/)

Unresolved launch due diligence includes applicable platform obligations, privacy/document handling, restricted goods, category permissions, brand authorization, and whether particular factory stock is eligible for domestic sale. This review does not establish legal compliance.

## Revenue hypotheses

For a network where members can conclude business directly, business subscriptions and advertising fit the initial model better than assuming every deal generates a commission.

| Option | Value to test | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Free core network | Participation, posting, discovery, and normal conversations | Needs a credible route to sustained revenue |
| Optional business subscription | Enquiry organization, richer profiles, analytics, team inbox | Suppliers will need evidence of useful business outcomes |
| Labelled promoted posts | Relevant exposure within categories or locations | Excessive promotion can reduce feed quality |
| Later transaction services | Payment, delivery, or order assistance where users request it | Costs, disputes, and settlement complexity |

IndiaMART’s disclosures provide a reference for supplier subscriptions and enquiry tools. Faire provides a different reference: commission-funded transaction services, with 0% commission on qualifying Faire Direct orders. Neither establishes an appropriate Bizzbook price. [IndiaMART disclosure](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/INDIAMART_20052025191807_St_Exchange_Submission_of_AGM_Notice_and_AR.pdf), [Faire brand help](https://www.faire.com/support/brands)

Do not set revenue forecasts from user registrations alone. Subscription revenue depends on paying businesses and price; advertising needs relevant audience activity and measurable advertiser value.

## Four-to-six-week validation proposal

The following sample sizes are practical research targets, not statistical benchmarks.

**Discovery:** Interview approximately 10 factory/manufacturer representatives, 10 wholesalers, and 20 entrepreneurs or small retailers. Ask about their last real purchase or sales lead, how they found the other party, what information was missing, and what happened next. With consent, inspect examples of posts and enquiries. Include users outside the founder’s immediate network and participants outside Dhaka.

**Pilot:** Recruit approximately 20–30 suppliers and 60–100 prospective buyers into a small feed-and-DM experience. Help suppliers publish genuine stock or capability posts; help buyers publish genuine requirements. Track assisted activity separately so staff effort does not look like organic demand.

**Evaluate:** Observe whether people find suitable contacts, receive replies, return, and report useful outcomes. Ask participants who stop using it why they stopped. Test willingness to pay only after users have experienced value. These activities are proposed; no interviews or pilot have been conducted for this report.

Priority questions:

- What makes someone open Bizzbook when their contacts already use Facebook or WhatsApp?
- Which posts produce relevant enquiries rather than only likes?
- Can manufacturers serve the quantities that interested entrepreneurs request?
- Do suppliers reply to beginners, and what makes an enquiry credible?
- Which identity signals do buyers understand and trust?
- Do conversations continue on Bizzbook, and why do they move elsewhere?
- Will suppliers pay for demonstrated business value, and what do they compare that cost with?

## Measures and decision criteria

A useful early diagnostic is **weekly active business pairs having a qualified two-way conversation**. Count distinct pairs, not messages. Define a qualified conversation as one where both sides respond and discuss a specific offer, requirement, or business opportunity; audit a consented sample to check the definition. Round 2 recommends evaluating this alongside buyer retention and verified useful outcomes, because conversation volume alone cannot establish durable value.

Supporting measures: first-week activation, response rate/time, relevant enquiries per active supplier, four-week retention by role/category, repeat conversations, spam/report rates, moderation time, and self-reported sample requests or purchases. Label self-reported outcomes separately from independently confirmed ones.

Set numeric continuation thresholds before the pilot, based on the available budget and recruitment method. Continue when useful interactions repeat without constant staff prompting. Rework the proposition if activity consists mostly of passive browsing, unanswered DMs, incompatible order sizes, or suppliers broadcasting to one another without buyer response.

## Market sizing and remaining uncertainty

No defensible Bizzbook market-size number has been established. General Facebook user counts, all SMEs, and national retail expenditure do not directly measure reachable active business-network users. BBS provides the Economic Census 2024 final report and sector tables as a starting point for a later estimate. [BBS census publication page](https://bbs.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/695b99a3c4774958d7b705e2)

Build a bottom-up estimate from reachable businesses in selected communities, relevant participants per business, observed activation/retention, and measured willingness to pay. Keep registered members, active members, paying businesses, transaction value, and platform revenue separate.

The research supports testing a Bangladesh business social network. Its decisive unanswered question is whether a relevant feed and trusted direct relationships create a recurring business benefit strong enough for users to adopt Bizzbook alongside their existing networks.
