# Bizzbook research, round 2: adoption and lasting business value

Date: 15 November 2026. Geography: Bangladesh. Product: an all-category, Facebook-like network for people doing business, including manufacturers, wholesalers, and new entrepreneurs.

## What changed in this round

The first report established relevant behaviour and outlined a possible product. This round investigates reasons the concept could struggle, closer precedents, and what evidence would justify building further.

The central assessment is more conditional: **Bizzbook needs to demonstrate recurring value for the people discovering and buying, alongside a reason for suppliers to publish.** A populated feed and enthusiastic suppliers would be encouraging but incomplete evidence.

This is desk research. No customers have been interviewed, no supplier has been contacted, no logged-in competitor experience has been tested, and no demand or willingness to pay has been validated. Recommendations and examples below are original hypotheses, not observed Bizzbook behaviour.

## 1. A close precedent: Anar

Anar’s founder described a small-business network with business profiles, a feed of products and requirements, discovery, and business communication. That is a much closer product reference than a conventional checkout-led wholesale marketplace. [Founder’s original thesis, 9 November 2021](https://medium.com/anar-business/anar-why-we-are-doing-what-we-are-doing-162b4a0dc56f)

In his 24 November 2023 closure account, the founder reported 1.5 million registrations but insufficient buyer value and persistent retention problems, despite increasing conversations and trying transaction services. These are retrospective founder claims, not independently audited operating data. His wider conclusions about Indian B2B trade are hypotheses to investigate in Bangladesh. [Founder’s retrospective](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/so-long-anar-farewell-reflection-nishank-jain-oifxf)

The company’s current site confirms that the journey ended in November 2023. It is a historical case, not a service to join today. [Anar website](https://www.anar.biz/)

Implication for our research design: recruit buyers independently, observe their real requirements, and track usefulness after the first introduction. Ask what they return for when a supplier has already been found. Treat conversation counts as intermediate signals. One failed company cannot establish that Bizzbook will fail, and Bangladesh-specific advantages must be measured rather than assumed.

## 3. What Bangladesh adoption studies actually tell us

**Useful and easy participation deserves testing.** Sarker and colleagues’ 2025 journal article analysed 402 Bangladeshi social-commerce users. The model linked usefulness, ease, and trust with attitudes, and included social support and grievance redressal among usage-related factors. Data were collected in 2022 through convenience/snowball recruitment; the sample was mostly urban and relatively educated. It studies consumers, not factory-to-retailer sourcing, and associations do not establish causal effects for Bizzbook. [Paper and methodology, pp. 1 and 6](https://cronfa.swan.ac.uk/Record/cronfa68587/Download/68587__33436__f85b86f325d54ebf8a6a056dfea980c3.pdf)

**Existing networks contribute value beyond the interface.** A paper published online in September 2026 reports qualitative work with 45 campus entrepreneurs from five Bangladeshi public universities. Its abstract identifies connectedness, knowledge sharing, trust within networks, interaction, and cashless transactions among growth themes. Only the abstract was reviewed. Its received/accepted dates are 2023/2024, so publication year should not be read as the year of fieldwork. [Publisher abstract](https://www.inderscience.com/info/inarticle.php?artid=156557)

Our inference: a familiar-looking interface can reduce learning effort, but Bizzbook must also offer relevant people, credible opportunities, and useful responses. A new app does not automatically inherit the relationships that make an existing community valuable.

**Access and use require separate consideration.** GSMA’s 2026 report discusses affordability, skills, and gender differences in mobile adoption; its Bangladesh findings include women who own smartphones but do not use mobile internet. This supports investigating actual devices and usage among recruits. It is not evidence that Bizzbook’s intended users have these same characteristics. [GSMA report](https://www.gsma.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/The-Mobile-Gender-Gap-Report-2026.pdf)

## 4. Separate the audience into real purchasing situations

The following segments are proposed for interviews. Their relative size and attractiveness are unknown.

| Situation | What the person may need | What would count as useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Exploring entrepreneurship, no immediate purchase | Learn categories, costs, and possible suppliers | Returns for specific business learning or progresses to a real requirement |
| Ready to make a first stock purchase | A credible supplier, feasible quantity, sample, and total cost | Obtains an applicable quote and takes a concrete next step |
| Active reseller or small retailer | Restocks, alternatives, new arrivals, dependable supply | Returns at the next relevant sourcing cycle |
| Launching a custom product or brand | Production capability, specifications, sampling, lead times | Finds a supplier able to discuss the actual production brief |
| Factory/manufacturer representative | Qualified opportunities compatible with capabilities | Replies to suitable enquiries and continues posting without constant help |
| Wholesaler/distributor | Repeat buyers and efficient handling of enquiries | Receives enquiries that justify the time spent publishing/responding |

One account can occupy several situations. A business owner may buy inputs and sell finished goods. New entrepreneurs may have intent without documents, inventory, or an immediate budget. Research these differences without treating all registrations as equivalent demand.

## 5. Reasons users might return

These are hypotheses to test individually:

- **Fresh supply:** relevant new arrivals, restocks, available stock lots, and updated capabilities.
- **Fresh demand:** genuine buyer requests with quantities, locations, and intended purchase timing.
- **Relationships:** responses from followed suppliers, useful introductions, and repeat business contacts.
- **Business learning:** practical questions and answers connected to an actual commercial decision.
- **Saved context:** revisiting products, quotes, prior discussions, and suppliers without searching through unrelated content.

The key is frequency appropriate to the business. Machinery sourcing and frequent stock replenishment may have different natural cycles. Weekly use is not automatically the right success criterion for every category.

Record why a user leaves. Finding a dependable supplier and continuing directly may be a successful introduction but weak evidence of sustained app engagement. Failing to find a suitable offer is a different outcome. Both affect the business model differently.

## 6. An all-category app still needs relevant local networks

Preserve the broad product vision while measuring whether each participant has enough suitable people and offers nearby or able to serve them.

For a single requirement, match at least: product/capability, order quantity, stock or production model, delivery destination, timing, and commercial terms. A large national member count can hide an empty experience for a particular buyer.

Proposed diagnostic: **requirement coverage** = genuine buyer requirements receiving at least one compatible supplier response within the agreed observation window ÷ genuine requirements posted in that cohort. Record whether compatibility is buyer-confirmed or only staff-assessed.

Examples for testing, not real listings: a buyer seeking 24 ready-stock pieces and a buyer requesting 2,000 custom pieces may belong in the same apparel community, but need different offers. Product relevance alone would not match them effectively.

Recruit a few connected communities across the categories the founder can credibly access. Report results by community and requirement type. This limits recruitment dispersion without changing the long-term all-category scope.

## 7. Public discovery and private commercial information

Test what businesses will publish openly. Candidate public information includes capabilities, selected products, approximate prices, minimum quantities, and general location. Candidate private information includes negotiated prices, credit arrangements, detailed specifications, and contact details.

Possible research prototype: public business posts and profiles, DMs for negotiation, and optional audience controls. Test whether controls help participants share useful information or create too much friction. These are design proposals, not a claim that all suppliers want secrecy.

Watch for repetitive catalogue uploads, expired offers, and sellers responding to unrelated buyer requests. Candidate controls include stock/status updates, request expiry, category relevance, duplicate reduction, and limits on unsolicited first contacts. Measure whether the controls improve response quality rather than assuming more posting always helps.

## 8. Trust has several independent layers

Evaluate them separately:

1. **Identity:** Who is the person, and are they authorized to represent this business?
2. **Capability:** Can the supplier provide the specified product or production service?
3. **Offer accuracy:** Is the stock, price, sample, or lead-time claim current and credible?
4. **Interaction quality:** Do they answer relevant questions and communicate reliably?
5. **Commercial outcome:** Was the agreed product supplied on the agreed terms?

A phone confirmation addresses only a narrow identity signal. Followers or reactions cannot establish fulfilment quality. For off-platform deals, transaction claims may be unverified; show that distinction honestly.

Bangladesh’s Bonik Somiti preprint raises concerns about false complaints, retaliation, biased administration, and exposure of users’ identities. Its prototype findings suggest private evidence review and accountable moderation deserve testing. It does not establish an operationally proven remedy. [Full paper, findings and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12650v1)

Practical hypotheses: let members report privately; explain moderation decisions; allow correction/appeal; distinguish business endorsements from purchase-backed reviews; avoid presenting an unresolved allegation as established fact. Test comprehension of verification labels with buyers and sellers.

## 9. Adoption alongside existing channels

The first adoption experiment should ask whether Bizzbook can add value while participants continue using their existing tools. Full migration is a separate, stronger claim.

Proposed flow: publish a genuine Bizzbook business post → owner voluntarily shares its link through an existing channel → recipient previews relevant content → chooses to follow or ask a question → receives a useful response → returns for another business purpose.

Measure drop-off at each step. Public previews and simple onboarding may reduce initial effort, but the repeat visit still needs its own reason. Compare voluntary owner-sharing with researcher-assisted recruitment so an acquisition method is not mistaken for organic demand.

Candidate research access points include existing business relationships, market associations, training cohorts, and industry member directories. BGMEA publishes a member-search interface and BKMEA lists a member section. These help identify potential research populations; membership alone does not establish current availability, domestic-sales suitability, or willingness to serve beginners. [BGMEA member search](https://bgmea.com.bd/page/member-search), [BKMEA member section](https://www.bkmea.com/public/member?Index=d)

## 10. Revenue research

Test payment for a demonstrated benefit. Possible benefits include enquiry organization, a team inbox, analytics, or relevant promotion. Keep basic participation sufficiently useful to observe whether a community develops.

For subscriptions, measure paid conversion, renewal, cancellations, support burden, and value by supplier type. For promotions, measure qualified responses and repeat advertiser use. For transaction services, investigate demand and operating costs separately before adding promises about protection or settlement.

Distinguish a person saying they would pay from an actual permitted purchase of a real, clearly described service. A seller willing to pay for exposure cannot establish buyer retention. Selling verification as if payment proves trust would also make trust signals hard to interpret.

No Bizzbook price, advertising yield, acquisition cost, or revenue forecast is supported by the research so far.

## 11. Evidence gaps and experiments

| Hypothesis | Present confidence | Proposed test | Evidence that would challenge it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Suppliers will post repeatedly | Unknown for Bizzbook | Observe voluntary posting after initial onboarding help ends | Publishing stops without reminders |
| Buyers find compatible offers | Unknown | Use real requirements and buyer-confirmed response suitability | Many replies but few usable offers |
| Buyers return for ongoing value | Unknown; highest priority | Follow cohorts through more than one relevant business cycle | Successful users leave permanently and no other recurring use appears |
| Trust cues improve decisions | Plausible, unvalidated here | Ask users to interpret cues during realistic supplier choices | Users misunderstand labels or ignore them |
| An all-category feed can stay relevant | Unknown | Observe task completion and irrelevant exposure by community | Broad volume grows while individual usefulness falls |
| Business subscriptions can work | Unknown | Offer a real pilot service after showing its value | Verbal interest produces no purchase or renewal |

“Plausible” means suitable for testing, not established demand. See the accompanying [field research guide](field-research-guide.md) for neutral prompts, recruitment targets, and a measurement protocol.

## 12. How this changes the first report

Retain its social-network direction, role flexibility, contextual DMs, and broad category vision. Treat the feature list as candidate research material rather than a validated specification. Upgrade buyer retention and demonstrated usefulness to first-class decision criteria. Keep conversation counts as diagnostics and record assistance and self-reported outcomes separately.

No source reviewed establishes the current number of Bangladesh businesses willing to use this specific product. Official directories, government portals, and general social-commerce studies cannot supply that denominator. National user totals, platform registrations, and revenue should remain separate quantities.

The next decision should come from observing real business requirements and repeat behaviour. Additional desk research is useful for specific gaps, especially local supplier practices and category economics; it cannot replace those observations.

## Source quality and access notes

- The Anar founding essay and closure post are first-person accounts. The website independently corroborates the closure announcement, but not the reported performance metrics.
- Current Alignable, WhatsApp, and other product pages establish advertised functionality, not business effectiveness or profitability.
- WENP’s homepage could not be retrieved reliably; the official 2023 manual was accessible. No current usage count is asserted.
- WEHelp and Shojib Connect public pages were reviewed without signing in. Listed capabilities were not exercised.
- The Sarker paper was available in full through a university repository. Its collection year and sample composition materially limit generalization.
- The campus entrepreneurship study was reviewed through its publisher abstract only.
- Bonik Somiti is an exploratory preprint and prototype evaluation, not nationwide evidence.
- GSMA’s report was available through indexed publisher excerpts; a direct PDF open was blocked. No numerical Bangladesh adoption estimate is used here.
- A linked Elevation investment memo could not be retrieved. No conclusions were inferred from its title.
